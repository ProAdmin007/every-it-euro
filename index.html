More than a technical decision

IT procurement has more than one dimension

Procurement is usually treated as a technical, security and financial decision. It is also an economic, sovereignty, supply-chain and strategic decision — whether or not that is written into the tender.

Technical

Does it work, scale and integrate? Is it supported for its full lifecycle?

Security

Is it well engineered, maintained and patched? Who can access it, and from where?

Financial

What is the total cost of ownership, including migration and exit?

Economic

Which companies, jobs and skills does this spending help build — and where?

Sovereignty

Who ultimately controls the technology, the data and the updates — and under which law?

Supply chain

What happens if the supplier is acquired, sanctioned or withdraws a product or service?

Strategic

Does this decision increase or reduce Europe's ability to choose for itself in ten years?

Our position

European preference — where it makes strategic, economic, security and technical sense

This website does not argue for blind protectionism, and it is not anti-American. Credibility matters more than ideology.

Where your IT budget goes matters.

What we argue

  • Procurement shapes where expertise, R&D, jobs and intellectual property accumulate.
  • Geographic, legal, technological and economic dependency belong in risk assessments.
  • Credible European options exist in many categories and deserve serious evaluation.

What we do not argue

  • That American or other non-European technology is bad or insecure.
  • That European products are automatically more secure or better.
  • That everything should be replaced — switching carries real cost and risk.
