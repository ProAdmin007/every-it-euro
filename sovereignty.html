Technology Sovereignty Assessment

Answer 15 questions about one specific system, such as your EDR platform, identity provider or SCADA historian. You get a classification and an explanation of why it was produced.

How to read the outcome

The outcome follows a small set of published rules. It is not a scientific score, and it does not say whether a product is good or bad. It shows how much weight sovereignty deserves in your decision. Your answers stay in your browser: nothing is stored or sent anywhere.

Part 1 — How important is the system?

Is this system business critical?

Would your core processes stop or degrade seriously without it?

Does it process sensitive information?

Personal data, trade secrets, classified or security-relevant information.

Is it part of critical infrastructure or an essential service?

For example energy, water, telecom, transport, health or public administration — including entities in scope of NIS2.

Is it connected to OT or ICS?

Industrial control systems, SCADA, building automation or other operational technology.

Would a supplier outage affect operations?

Think of a multi-day outage of the supplier's cloud, support or licensing service.

Is it a security control that protects other systems?

Examples: EDR, firewall, identity, PAM, SIEM, backup. These usually hold privileged access.

Part 2 — How dependent are you on the supplier?

Does it require continuous access to the supplier's cloud?

Including cloud-only management consoles and licence checks.

Can it run independently of the supplier?

Could you keep it running for months without the supplier's involvement?

Can your organisation export all data and configuration?

In documented, usable formats — not only as a vendor-specific backup.

Are open standards used?

Open protocols, file formats and documented APIs that other products also support.

Is there a viable European alternative?

Check the European alternatives database. “Viable” means technically and operationally realistic for you.

Which jurisdiction ultimately controls the supplier?

The law that applies to the company that makes the final decisions — not the local sales office.

Where is the ultimate parent company located?

Follow the ownership chain to the top, including private-equity owners.

Could export restrictions or sanctions affect the technology?

Including restrictions on updates, licences, support or underlying components.

Does the supplier have remote access, push updates or receive telemetry?

Any channel through which the supplier can change or observe the system.

How the classification works

Each answer counts as an importance factor (Part 1), a dependency factor (Part 2) or a mitigating factor. “Not sure” is treated as a cautious “yes” for importance and as a dependency, and is listed as an open question for you to resolve. The rules are applied from top to bottom:

Classification rules of the Technology Sovereignty Assessment
OutcomeRule
Strategic technologyCritical infrastructure or OT/ICS, and business critical or outage-sensitive, and 3 or more dependency factors — or 5 or more importance factors together with 5 or more dependency factors.
High relevance3 or more importance factors with 2 or more dependency factors — or critical infrastructure/OT with at least 1 dependency factor — or a security control with 3 or more dependency factors.
Medium relevanceAt least 1 importance factor with at least 1 dependency factor — or 3 or more importance factors — or 4 or more dependency factors.
Low relevanceNone of the above.

Limitations

  • The thresholds are editorial judgements, chosen to be simple and explainable. They are not validated statistically.
  • The tool does not assess technical security, quality or cost. Those remain essential.
  • Use the outcome to decide how much attention sovereignty needs, then do the work with the procurement checklist.
