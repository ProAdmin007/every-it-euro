Worked example

€50,000 for firewalls: where does the money go?

The same budget spent in two ways — on Fortinet FortiGate or on Stormshield Network Security firewalls — traced through each vendor's own published annual figures.

Read this first
  • This is an illustration, not a price or quality comparison. The same budget can buy different capacity, features and support from each vendor. Nothing here says which firewall is better or more secure.
  • How the vendor's share is split: each company's whole-company figures for its latest financial year, applied proportionally to the money the vendor receives. A real purchase has its own mix of hardware, subscriptions and support.
  • The channel share is an assumption. What distributors, resellers and integrators keep is not published. Adjust it below.
  • Different levels of disclosure. Fortinet, a listed US company, publishes a full income statement. Stormshield, a French subsidiary of the Airbus group, only publishes its revenue and net result in the French business register. Its breakdown is therefore coarser — a difference in disclosure, not in substance.

What this shows — and what it does not

What it shows

  • In both cases, a meaningful share of the budget stays with European distributors, resellers and integrators.
  • With Fortinet, the vendor's share largely funds manufacturing in Asia, development in North America and profit that is mainly booked in the United States — while Fortinet also employs many people in Europe.
  • With Stormshield, the vendor's share funds a French company with its staff in France, and its profit remains within a European group.
  • Control differs as well: the jurisdiction that applies to the vendor, where the code is written and who decides on updates.

What it does not show

  • Which firewall is more secure, faster or better suited to your network.
  • How much capacity, functionality or support €50,000 buys from each vendor.
  • Total cost of ownership, migration effort or the skills available in your organisation.
  • What happens further down the chain — suppliers, subcontractors and shareholders of either company.
