Worked example

Network security: how much of your money comes back to Europe?

When a European organisation buys network security, part of the money pays people in Europe, ends up as taxes and social contributions in Europe and stays with European owners — or it does not. This example follows the money using the 2025 annual reports of secunet (Germany) and Fortinet (United States), two listed companies that both publish full figures.

Read this first
  • A comparison of companies, not of products. secunet's main product, SINA, is a family of encrypted network gateways and clients approved for classified government networks; Fortinet's FortiGate is a general-purpose firewall. Both protect network traffic and both offer encrypted VPN connections, but they rarely compete for the same contract. Nothing here says which product is more secure or better for your network.
  • Different customers and sizes. secunet had revenue of €458.8 million in 2025, 89% of it in Germany and about €295 million with its largest customer; 89.8% came from government, defence and similar organisations. Fortinet had revenue of $6.8 billion worldwide.12
  • No invented prices. Neither company publishes a price list. The cost calculator works with your own quotes.
  • Published figures only. All figures come from the companies' own 2025 annual reports and documentation, or are derived from them, as marked.

Part 1

What comes back to Europe

Of every €100 each company received in 2025: how much went to people, taxes and owners in Europe — and how much went elsewhere.

Staff costs, all in Germany

€26.53

of every €100 secunet received: €22.73 salaries, €3.74 social contributions and €0.05 company pensions. All of its 1,302 employees work in Germany.1

To the German state

€7.80

of every €100 secunet received: €4.06 corporate income tax and €3.74 social contributions. By comparison, all income taxes Fortinet owed outside the United States together came to €0.80 of every €100.12

Profit for European owners

€7.26

of every €100 secunet received was net profit. It proposes to pay €3.64 of it as dividends; three quarters of the shares belong to Giesecke+Devrient, a family-owned company in Munich.13

In both cases, part of the price stays with your supplier

Network security is usually bought through a distributor and a reseller or integrator. For a European buyer these are usually European companies, whichever vendor you choose. Their share is not published.

In short: of every €100 paid to secunet, €26.53 went to staff in Germany, €7.80 to the German state and €7.26 became profit for mainly German owners. Fortinet also employs many people in Europe, but its development, most of its manufacturing and most of its profit are outside Europe. Where secunet's €58.36 of materials and purchased services is spent is not fully published.

The purchase price is only part of the story. Fortinet earned 67% of its 2025 revenue from services such as subscriptions and support; secunet's product revenue includes maintenance and support.23 Over the years, you keep paying the vendor — and keep depending on it.

If you want to switch later

  • The configuration has to be migrated. Policies, VPN configurations and objects are vendor-specific. Plan for conversion, testing and a period of running both in parallel.
  • The hardware usually goes too. Appliances run the vendor's own software. Ask whether hardware can be reused or traded in.
  • Prepaid subscriptions run on. Multi-year terms may not be refundable when you switch early. Align term ends with your exit plan.
  • Skills, approvals and tooling change. Training, monitoring integrations and — for classified networks — security approvals are product-specific.

The procurement checklist covers exit plans, data and configuration export and change-of-control clauses.

Part 3

What if something goes wrong?

Five things that can happen during the life of a network security product, with real precedents and what they mean for each company. They are not predictions.

1. Export restrictions or sanctions

What happens: a vendor may be legally required, or decide, to stop sales, support and updates in a country or for certain customers.

Precedent: in March 2022 Fortinet suspended sales, support and professional services in Russia.5 Cisco stopped all business operations in Russia and Belarus and later refunded prepaid service and software arrangements.6

Fortinet: products with encryption are subject to US export controls and “may be exported outside the United States only with the required export license or through an export license exception, or may be prohibited altogether from export to certain countries.”2

secunet: states that there are “restrictions regarding eligible customers and regions for products subject to export restrictions, foreign trade regulations, and national confidentiality”, primarily for SINA products.3 For a buyer in Germany or the EU, these are rules of their own jurisdiction.

2. The vendor is acquired

What happens: a new owner can change the roadmap, licensing and prices — or retire the product line.

Precedent: Sophos acquired Cyberoam in 2014, announced the end of sales in 2019 and moved customers to its own firewall.7 Sophos UTM reached end of life on 30 June 2026: no licence renewals after that date, no support after 31 December 2026.8

Fortinet: a large listed company.

secunet: listed, with a controlling shareholder: Giesecke+Devrient (Munich), which is family-owned, holds 75.12%. A change of control would require its agreement.1 European security companies that came under non-European ownership are listed under ownership changes to watch.

Contract: a change-of-control clause, price protection for renewals and a right to export your configuration.

3. Support and updates end

What happens: every model and software version reaches end of support. After that there are no security patches — for network security, a serious risk.

Fortinet: hardware support generally ends 60 months after end of order; software versions get 36 + 18 months, long-term-support versions up to 72.4

secunet: support periods are not published in the sources used for this example.

What counts is the date for the exact model you buy. Ask for it in writing.

4. The subscription is not renewed

What happens: because you choose to stop, cannot pay, or the vendor can no longer deliver.

Fortinet: “The FortiGate will still function as a firewall if any or all of the FortiGuard licenses are expired.” Antivirus and intrusion prevention keep working on their last signatures without updates; category-based web and DNS filtering stops, and by default that traffic is then blocked.9

secunet: not published in the sources used for this example; ask what happens to the product when support ends.

5. The vendor runs into financial difficulty

What happens: a vendor in difficulty may cut development or support, be sold or, at worst, cease trading.

Fortinet: net income of $1.85 billion in 2025 and $7.1 billion of deferred revenue — subscriptions and support already invoiced but still to be delivered.2

secunet: net income of €33.3 million in 2025, an equity ratio of 40.7% and an order book of €278.9 million at year end. It depends heavily on public-sector customers in Germany.1

What this shows — and what it does not

What it shows

  • Money paid to a European vendor largely comes back to Europe: as salaries for people in Europe, as taxes and social contributions to a European state, and as profit for European owners.
  • Money paid to Fortinet also pays European jobs, mainly in sales and support, but its development, most of its manufacturing and most of its profit are outside Europe.
  • Most of what you pay over the years is often recurring, and network security depends on its vendor for updates — the relationship matters more than the purchase price.
  • A European vendor is not automatically the better product, and has its own risks — such as dependence on a few large customers.

What it does not show

  • Which product is more secure or better suited to your network. secunet and Fortinet mostly serve different needs.
  • Actual prices: neither company publishes them.
  • Where secunet's purchased materials and services are produced.
  • Other vendors. secunet and Fortinet were chosen because both publish full annual reports and secunet is profitable and pays tax in Europe — not because they are the only or the best options.
