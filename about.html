About this website

An independent, non-commercial resource for people who buy, secure and govern technology in Europe.

Purpose

European organisations spend very large sums on IT and cybersecurity every year. This website argues that this spending is not only a technical, security and financial decision, but also an economic and strategic one. It aims to help CISOs, CTOs, security architects, OT security professionals, procurement teams, public bodies, critical infrastructure operators and policymakers add one more question to their decisions:

What are the strategic consequences of this procurement decision for Europe's technological capability?

Editorial principles

  • European preference, not protectionism. We advocate European options where they make strategic, economic, security and technical sense — not at any price.
  • Not anti-American. Non-European suppliers employ many people in Europe, pay taxes here and often offer excellent technology. Our argument concerns dependency and capability, not nationality as such.
  • Trade-offs in the open. Switching suppliers costs money and carries risk. Some European products are less mature. We say so.
  • Regulation is not preference. We separate what the law requires from what is a strategic choice. No EU law we cite requires organisations to buy European technology.
  • No fabricated statistics. Where reliable figures are not available, we use conceptual diagrams and say clearly that they are conceptual.

Methodology

Sources

We prioritise primary sources: EUR-Lex, the European Commission, ENISA, the European Parliament and Council, national cybersecurity authorities, court rulings, and official company websites. Reputable news reporting is used only for events. Every source is listed with its organisation, publication date where known and date of access on the Sources page (57 sources).

Company database

Each entry in the European Technology Alternatives database was checked against the company's own website (imprint, about and investor pages) and, for ownership changes, reputable reporting. The URLs used are listed in each entry. We check headquarters, parent company, ownership, current existence and product relevance, and apply the published classification criteria. Companies that are defunct, or that came under non-European control, are excluded from the main list. Relevant acquisitions are shown under ownership changes to watch.

Inclusion is not an endorsement, and we have no commercial relationship with any company listed. Ownership changes, so every entry carries a “last verified” date.

Tools

The Technology Sovereignty Assessment uses simple published rules rather than a weighted score, so that every outcome can be explained and challenged. The €100 explorer is qualitative by design and contains no invented percentages.

Corrections and suggestions

Facts change and mistakes happen. If you find an error, an outdated ownership record or a missing European company, please open an issue in the GitHub repository of this website and include a link to an official source. Issue templates are provided for corrections and new companies. Suppliers are welcome to submit corrections about themselves, with sources.

Privacy

  • No cookies, no analytics, no tracking pixels, no advertising.
  • No external fonts, scripts or style sheets. Everything is served from this website.
  • The interactive tools run entirely in your browser. Answers are not stored or transmitted.
  • The site opens in your device's language when a translation is available. If you pick a language in the language switcher, only that choice is saved in your browser's local storage so the site opens in that language next time. It is never sent anywhere; clearing this website's data in your browser removes it.
  • A strict Content Security Policy only allows resources from this website.

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub may process technical data such as IP addresses in server logs; see GitHub's own privacy statement. External links to company websites and sources lead to sites with their own privacy policies.

Accessibility

We aim to meet WCAG 2.2 level AA: semantic HTML, keyboard operability, visible focus states, sufficient colour contrast, reduced-motion support, labelled form controls, and content that remains readable without JavaScript. If something does not work for you, please report it as described above.
