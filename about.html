About this website

An independent resource for people who buy, secure and govern technology in Europe.

Purpose

European organisations spend very large sums on IT and cybersecurity every year. This website argues that this spending is not only a technical, security and financial decision, but also an economic and strategic one. It aims to help CISOs, CTOs, security architects, OT security professionals, procurement teams, public bodies, critical infrastructure operators and policymakers add one more question to their decisions:

What are the strategic consequences of this procurement decision for Europe's technological capability?

Editorial principles

  • European preference, not protectionism. We advocate European options where they make strategic, economic, security and technical sense — not at any price.
  • Not anti-American. Non-European suppliers employ many people in Europe, pay taxes here and often offer excellent technology. Our argument concerns dependency and capability, not nationality as such.
  • Trade-offs in the open. Switching suppliers costs money and carries risk. Some European products are less mature. We say so.
  • Regulation is not preference. We separate what the law requires from what is a strategic choice. No EU law we cite requires organisations to buy European technology.
  • No fabricated statistics. Where reliable figures are not available, we use conceptual diagrams and say clearly that they are conceptual.

Methodology

Sources

We prioritise primary sources: EUR-Lex, the European Commission, ENISA, the European Parliament and Council, national cybersecurity authorities, court rulings, and official company websites. Reputable news reporting is used only for events. Every source is listed with its organisation, publication date where known and date of access on the Sources page (66 sources).

Company database

Each entry in the European Technology Alternatives database was checked against the company's own website (imprint, about and investor pages) and, for ownership changes, reputable reporting. The URLs used are listed in each entry. We check headquarters, parent company, ownership, current existence and product relevance, and apply the published classification criteria. Companies that are defunct, or that came under non-European control, are excluded from the main list. Relevant acquisitions are shown under ownership changes to watch.

Inclusion is not an endorsement, and we have no commercial relationship with any company listed; see funding and advertising. Ownership changes, so every entry carries a “last verified” date.

Tools

The Technology Sovereignty Assessment uses simple published rules rather than a weighted score, so that every outcome can be explained and challenged. The €100 explorer is qualitative by design and contains no invented percentages.

Corrections and suggestions

Facts change and mistakes happen. If you find an error, an outdated ownership record or a missing European company, please open an issue in the GitHub repository of this website and include a link to an official source. Issue templates are provided for corrections and new companies. Suppliers are welcome to submit corrections about themselves, with sources.

Funding and advertising

Running this website costs money (server, domain and the time to keep the database current). It is funded by advertising through Google AdSense and by voluntary donations through Ko-fi.

  • Advertising never influences the content. Inclusion, classification and order in the database, the assessment tool and every article are decided only by the editorial principles and the published methodology.
  • Google chooses the ads, not us. We do not sell advertising space directly. Where AdSense allows it, we block ads from the companies in our database and from IT security product categories, so ads are not mistaken for recommendations.
  • Companies cannot pay to be listed, to be listed higher or to be removed.
  • Ads are labelled “Advertisement” and kept apart from the content: in the margins on wide screens and once below the introduction on smaller screens.

Privacy and cookies

Hosting

The website runs on our own server at OVHcloud in France (EU). The web server does not keep access logs, so we do not store visitors' IP addresses. Only error messages are logged, for a limited time and size.

Visitor statistics: Simple Analytics

We count visits with Simple Analytics, a Dutch service that stores its data in the EU. It uses no cookies, does not store IP addresses and does not identify or follow individual visitors. We only see totals, such as page views, referring websites, countries and browser languages.

Advertising: Google AdSense

Ads are provided by Google AdSense. For visitors in the European Economic Area, Google Ireland Limited is responsible for the personal data it processes. Google and its advertising partners may use cookies and similar technologies to show ads, to limit how often you see the same ad, to measure ads and to prevent fraud.

  • Your consent comes first. On your first visit, Google's consent message asks whether you accept personalised ads and the related cookies. Ad cookies are only placed according to your choice.
  • If you decline, Google shows non-personalised or limited ads, based on the page you are reading instead of your interests.
  • You can change your choice at any time with the Cookie settings link at the bottom of every page.
  • How Google uses this information: How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services. You can manage personalised ads in Google's My Ad Center.

In your browser only

  • The interactive tools run entirely in your browser. Answers are not stored or transmitted.
  • The site opens in your device's language when a translation is available. If you pick a language in the language switcher, only that choice is saved in your browser's local storage so the site opens in that language next time. It is never sent anywhere; clearing this website's data in your browser removes it.
  • No external fonts or style sheets. Apart from Simple Analytics and Google's advertising and consent scripts, every file is served from this website.
  • A Content Security Policy blocks inline scripts and only allows encrypted (HTTPS) sources.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask for access to, correction or deletion of personal data, and you can lodge a complaint with your data protection authority. Because we do not store personal data ourselves, requests about advertising data are best sent to Google through the links above. For questions about this website, contact us at info@eu-techcompass.eu.

External links to company websites and sources lead to sites with their own privacy policies. Last updated: 19 November 2026.

Accessibility

We aim to meet WCAG 2.2 level AA: semantic HTML, keyboard operability, visible focus states, sufficient colour contrast, reduced-motion support, labelled form controls, and content that remains readable without JavaScript. If something does not work for you, please report it as described above.
