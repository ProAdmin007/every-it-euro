# 04 — Editorial principles

> **Every IT euro is also a strategic choice.**

## Position

- **European preference, not protectionism.** We advocate European options where they make strategic, economic, security and technical sense — not at any price.
- **Not anti-American.** Non-European suppliers employ many people in Europe, pay taxes here and often offer excellent technology. The argument concerns dependency and capability, not nationality as such.
- **Trade-offs in the open.** Switching suppliers costs money and carries risk. Some European products are less mature. We say so.
- **Regulation is not preference.** We separate what the law requires from what is a strategic choice. No EU law cited on the site requires organisations to buy European technology. Every law is presented as "Regulatory requirement" / "Strategic choice it can inform" / "Does it require buying European? No."
- **No fabricated statistics.** See [03 — Sources and evidence](03-sources-and-evidence.md).

## What the site argues — and what it does not

| We argue | We do not argue |
|---|---|
| Procurement shapes where expertise, R&D, jobs and intellectual property accumulate. | That American or other non-European technology is bad or insecure. |
| Geographic, legal, technological and economic dependency belong in risk assessments. | That European products are automatically more secure or better. |
| Credible European options exist in many categories and deserve serious evaluation. | That everything should be replaced — switching carries real cost and risk. |

## Sovereignty

The site distinguishes four kinds of sovereignty within digital sovereignty (see `why-europe.html`): data, technological, operational and economic sovereignty. EU hosting alone is not sovereignty: a non-European provider with data in an EU region may still be subject to non-European law with extraterritorial reach.

## Tone and wording

- Audience: CISOs, CTOs, security architects, OT security professionals, procurement teams, public bodies, critical infrastructure operators and policymakers. Professional, precise, calm.
- No alarmism, no ridicule of companies or countries, no superlatives.
- Comparisons between named suppliers are factual and symmetric: the same questions for both, the same standard of evidence, and an explicit statement of what the comparison does **not** show.
- "Relevant when evaluating …", never "replacement for …".

## Independence and money

- No advertising, tracking, cookies or third-party scripts (see the privacy section in `about.html`).
- No commercial relationship with any company listed. Any future funding (donations, sponsorship) must be disclosed and must not influence the database or the content.

## Not advice

The site is general information, not legal, procurement or security advice. Public procurement in particular is subject to strict rules; content about it must say what is generally permitted and point readers to their legal and procurement specialists.
